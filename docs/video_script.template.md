## 0:00–1:30 · Hook (slide 1–2) · P1

"Meet {{hypo_first}}, one of the virtual patients in our demo. {{hypo_pronoun_cap}} is {{hypo_age}}, lives in {{hypo_city}}, has type 2 diabetes and takes premixed insulin twice a day. On a busy morning, insulin taken and breakfast delayed, glucose can slide below 70 within two hours, and today nobody would know until {{hypo_pronoun}} felt shaky or confused.

India has {{india_dm}} adults with diabetes and {{india_pre}} more with prediabetes. Our care is built around a quarterly HbA1c: a number that tells us about the last three months but nothing about tonight. The risky moments, post-meal spikes, night-time lows, a viral fever, all happen between visits. CGMs and smartwatches now capture them, but no doctor can watch hundreds of glucose streams.

We built MadhuTwin to change that."

## 1:30–3:30 · What a digital twin is (slide 3) · P2

"MadhuTwin creates a living virtual replica of each patient. Three properties make it a twin and not just a prediction model.

**Model.** We simulate the person's glucose–insulin physiology: insulin sensitivity, beta-cell response, how fast they absorb an idli versus a plate of biryani, the dawn phenomenon, exercise, sleep, and the drugs Indian patients actually take: metformin, gliclazide, teneligliptin, dapagliflozin, premixed 30/70 insulin.

**Sync.** Every five minutes, an Unscented Kalman Filter updates the twin's hidden state from the latest CGM reading, including how the person's insulin sensitivity is drifting today.

**Simulate.** From that synced state we run the body forward: a strictly causal two-hour forecast, and what-if scenarios the doctor can try.

On top sits TwinNet, a neural network that fuses both data streams with the twin's own forecast and learns how much to trust physics at each horizon."

## 3:30–5:00 · Architecture (slide 4) · P2

"On the left, the two streams the challenge asks for. The static EHR: demographics, diagnoses coded in SNOMED CT, labs in LOINC including HbA1c, fasting glucose and insulin, two years of history, medications with timing, family history and genetic markers, the TCF7L2 risk allele and a polygenic score. The dynamic stream: CGM, heart rate, HRV, steps, sleep stages, and logged meals and doses.

In the middle, the twin core: ingest into one five-minute schema, then model, sync, simulate, then TwinNet. On the right, the clinician dashboard and a FHIR export that carries the forecast as a RiskAssessment to any hospital system. Governance runs across everything: consent, purpose-tagged audit, de-identified data."

## 5:00–7:00 · Data (slides 5–6) · P2

"We followed the sandbox rules strictly: no real Indian patient data. We used three sources.

First, a synthetic India-calibrated cohort of 1,000 patients over 14 days. The EHR follows Indian epidemiology and prescribing; meals come from the Indian Food Composition Tables: idli-sambar, roti-sabzi, ragi mudde, chai with sugar, Diwali sweets. The key point: the wearable streams are generated by the same simulated body, and the labs are measured from it. So when a synthetic patient's HbA1c is 8.4, it is because their simulated glucose averaged what HbA1c 8.4 means. The two streams are causally linked, which is what a fusion model needs to learn.

Second, CGMacros: 45 real people with two CGMs, a Fitbit, logged meals and lab tests. Third, ShanghaiT2DM: 100 patients from a different country, on a rice-based diet, many on insulin, and with no wearables at all. That is our external test."

## 7:00–9:00 · Model (slide 7) · P2

"Each twin is personalised from about a week of data, with priors from the EHR: fasting glucose and insulin give us a starting insulin sensitivity. When we fitted twins to the 45 real CGMacros participants, the twin's error over 1–4 hour windows fell from {{fit_prior}} to {{fit_pers}} mg/dL, {{fit_gain}} percent lower.

More importantly, the fitted parameters mean something. Without ever being told, the twin's insulin sensitivity tracks the lab HOMA-IR and its beta-cell response tracks HbA1c. That is our evidence this is physiology, not curve fitting. We then used these real-world relationships to generate the synthetic Indian cohort."

## 9:00–10:30 · Sync (slide 8) · P2

"Synchronisation keeps the twin alive. Besides glucose and insulin, our filter estimates a slowly drifting insulin-sensitivity factor. When someone catches a viral fever, insulin stops working as well. On unseen synthetic patients, the synced twin separates ill days from well days with an AUROC of {{ill_auroc}}: average sensitivity drops to {{ill_si_ill}} times baseline on ill days, versus {{ill_si_well}} on well days. The twin notices illness from CGM alone, without a single symptom being typed."

## 10:30–12:30 · TwinNet (slide 9) · P2

"TwinNet takes six hours of 21 channels through a causal temporal convolution; the EHR conditions those features through FiLM, so the same meal is read differently for a lean 30-year-old and an insulin-resistant 60-year-old; and the twin's own forecast enters through a gate.

That gate is interpretable. The network trusts physics {{gate30}} at 30 minutes and {{gate120}} at two hours: data-driven extrapolation is good short-term, physics is better long-term. We train with modality dropout so the same model works when wearables or the EHR are missing, and we calibrate the 80 percent band with conformal prediction so it is honest."

## 12:30–16:30 · Results (slides 10–14) · P1 and P2

**P2:** "Everything is evaluated on patients the models never saw, and for synthetic patients only on the second week, after personalisation. On {{n_test}} unseen patients, TwinNet's 60-minute error is {{tn_rmse60}} mg/dL, against {{pers_rmse60}} for assuming glucose stays flat: {{gain60}} percent lower. {{tn_clarkeAB60}} percent of 60-minute forecasts fall in the clinically acceptable zones of the Clarke Error Grid, and {{tn_cov60}} percent of outcomes land inside the 80 percent band."

**P1:** "For a doctor, what matters is the warning. TwinNet flags {{spike_detected}} percent of sustained spikes in advance, a median of {{spike_lead}} minutes before they start, with {{spike_fa}} false alert episodes per patient per day. Hypoglycaemia is discriminated with an AUROC of {{hypo_auroc}}. Every alert comes with its reasons."

**P2:** "The challenge asks us to fuse two streams, so we tested whether fusion matters. We retrained the same model on each combination. CGM alone: {{abl_cgm_rmse60}} mg/dL at 60 minutes. Both streams: {{abl_both_rmse60}}. Add the physics twin: {{abl_full_rmse60}}. Spike AUROC rises from {{abl_cgm_auroc}} to {{abl_full_auroc}}.

On real people, CGMacros with patient-level cross-validation, TwinNet pretrained on the synthetic Indian cohort and fine-tuned on real patients reaches {{cg_rmse60}} mg/dL at 60 minutes, versus {{cg_pers60}} for persistence, with {{cg_ab60}} percent in Clarke A+B. On ShanghaiT2DM, a different population with no wearables at all, it reaches {{sh_rmse60}} versus {{sh_pers60}}. Two lessons from real data: pretraining on our synthetic Indian cohort lifts spike detection on real people from an AUROC of {{cg_spike_real_only}} to {{cg_spike_s2r}}, but a model trained only on synthetic data does not transfer to a new population without a short fine-tune.

One more result for India. CGM sensors are expensive. In our CGM-light mode the patient wears a CGM for one week; then only four fingersticks a day and a smartwatch remain. The synced twin still estimates continuous glucose with {{light_mard}} percent mean error, against {{light_carry_mard}} percent for carrying the last fingerstick forward, and weekly time-in-range within ±{{light_tir}} percentage points. That result is on synthetic patients, so it is an upper bound we still need to confirm on real people."

**P1:** "We are honest about limits. Synthetic patients share the twin's structure, which flatters physics there; real and external data are the primary evidence; our real cohorts are small and not Indian. In CGMacros, the few lows occur mostly in people without diabetes and look like sensor artefacts, so hypoglycaemia performance on real data is not yet established. That is why our next step is a pilot in Indian clinics."

## 23:00–24:00 · Responsible design and roadmap (slides 18–19) · P1

"MadhuTwin is decision support, not an autopilot. It follows the DPDP Act: consent, purpose limitation, data minimisation and audit. It speaks ABDM's language: FHIR R4 with SNOMED CT and LOINC.
